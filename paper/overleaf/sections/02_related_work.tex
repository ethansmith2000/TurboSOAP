\section{2. Relationship to existing
work}\label{relationship-to-existing-work}}

Kronecker-factored methods approximate a large curvature or
gradient-covariance matrix with small per-dimension factors: K-FAC
approximates layerwise Fisher blocks as Kronecker products
\citep{martens2015optimizing}, Shampoo keeps one preconditioning matrix
per tensor dimension \citep{gupta2018shampoo}, and Shampoo has been
engineered for large-scale distributed training
\citep{anil2020scalable,shi2023distributed}. EKFAC tracks a diagonal
variance in a Kronecker-factored eigenbasis rather than in parameter
coordinates, which admits cheap partial updates \citep{george2018fast}.
SOAP does the analogous thing for Shampoo, running Adam \citep{kingma2014adam}
in the eigenbasis of Shampoo's preconditioner; because that basis is
recomputed only periodically, the second-moment average keeps adapting
in a slowly changing coordinate system between refreshes
\citep{vyas2024soap}. TurboSOAP keeps this structure and changes how
most of those refreshes are computed.

Vyas et al.~describe SOAP's rotated-coordinate adaptive updates and the
use of infrequent basis refinement: a warm-started power iteration
followed by QR (Algorithm 4). Thus both arms already reuse the preceding
basis. SOAP also discusses lower-precision state and the FP32 minimum
for its QR implementation (§§7.2.1, 7.3.1, 8). We retain the FP32 solver
island, use QR every 50 ages with intermediate basis tracking, and store
persistent matrix state in BF16. Our stock matrix memory settings, model
and auxiliary recipe are specified separately.
\citep{vyas2024soap}.

Milligan et al.~store the basis-coordinate preconditioner and use
subspace updates, including QR within selected blocks; they also study
BF16 storage. Our implementation instead stores world-coordinate
covariance factors and updates the full basis, coupling all coordinate
pairs without blocking or subspace restriction, with damped motion and
fixed QR repair. We do not maintain their persistent
basis-coordinate representation or establish a performance ranking
against it. \citep{milligan2026reparametrizing}, §§3.1--3.2.

Eschenhagen et al.~use approximation-error criteria to adapt eigenbasis
computation and examine the effects of stale bases. Our schedule is
fixed across factors and training stages; no residual controls refresh
decisions. Their analysis is relevant context, rather than a directly
measured comparator in this study.
\citep{eschenhagen2025purifying}, §4.

Warm reuse, subspace updates \citep{oja1982simplified,yang1995projection},
low-precision storage \citep{micikevicius2017mixed,kalamkar2019study},
and compilation \citep{ansel2024pytorch} are
existing ideas. The distinction studied here is the particular full-basis
tracking update, repair schedule, state history, and precision policy. Direct comparisons
with subspace \citep{milligan2026reparametrizing} or adaptive
\citep{eschenhagen2025purifying,nam2026foam} implementations remain outside this
experiment. Low-precision bases plus orthogonality correction are also
prior work: Wang et al.~quantize Shampoo eigenvector matrices and apply
Björck orthonormalization (§3.2). Our BF16 SOAP policy is not a
new instance of that general idea.
\citep{wang2024fourbit}. The
numerical ingredients also have classical lineage: Jacobi eigensolvers
use plane rotations \citep{forsythe1960cyclic}, and iterative polar orthogonalization predates this
work \citep{bjorck1971iterative,higham2008functions,jordan2024muon}.
