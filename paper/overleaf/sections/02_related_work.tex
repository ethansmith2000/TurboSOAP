\section{2. Relationship to existing
work}\label{relationship-to-existing-work}}

Vyas et al.~describe SOAP's rotated-coordinate adaptive updates and the
use of infrequent basis refinement: a warm-started power iteration
followed by QR (Algorithm 4). Thus both arms already reuse the preceding
basis. SOAP also discusses lower-precision state and the FP32 minimum
for its QR implementation (§§7.2.1, 7.3.1, 8). We retain the FP32 solver
island, use QR every 50 ages with intermediate dense motion, and store
persistent matrix state in BF16. Our stock matrix memory settings, model
and auxiliary recipe are specified separately.
\citep{vyas2024soap}.

Milligan et al.~store the basis-coordinate preconditioner and use
subspace updates, including QR within selected blocks; they also study
BF16 storage. Our implementation instead stores world-coordinate
covariance factors and updates the full basis with dense damped motion
and fixed QR repair. We do not maintain their persistent
basis-coordinate representation or establish a performance ranking
against it. \citep{milligan2026reparametrizing}, §§3.1--3.2.

Eschenhagen et al.~use approximation-error criteria to adapt eigenbasis
computation and examine the effects of stale bases. Our schedule is
fixed across factors and training stages; no residual controls refresh
decisions. Their analysis is relevant context, rather than a directly
measured comparator in this study.
\citep{eschenhagen2025purifying}, §4.

Warm reuse, subspace updates, low-precision storage, and compilation are
existing ideas. The distinction studied here is their particular dense
update, repair, state-history, and precision policy. Direct comparisons
with subspace or adaptive implementations remain outside this
experiment. Low-precision bases plus orthogonality correction are also
prior work: Wang et al.~quantize Shampoo eigenvector matrices and apply
Björck orthonormalization (§3.2). Our BF16 dense SOAP policy is not a
new instance of that general idea.
\citep{wang2024fourbit}. The
numerical ingredients also have classical lineage: Jacobi eigensolvers
use plane rotations \citep{forsythe1960cyclic}, and iterative polar orthogonalization predates this
work \citep{bjorck1971iterative}.
