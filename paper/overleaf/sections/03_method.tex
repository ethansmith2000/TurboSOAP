\hypertarget{method}{%
\section{3. Method}\label{method}}

\hypertarget{state-precision-and-update-ordering}{%
\subsection{3.1. State, precision, and update
ordering}\label{state-precision-and-update-ordering}}

For a matrix parameter \(W\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}\), the optimizer
stores first and second moments \(M,V\), covariance factors \(C_L,C_R\),
and square bases \(Q_L,Q_R\). All six persistent tensors use BF16.
Parameters, scalar age and learning-rate tensors, pointwise moment
temporaries, and decomposition inputs/outputs before basis storage use
FP32. Auxiliary parameters use the separate AdamW optimizer described in
Section 4.

Let \(R_b\) denote rounding to BF16. We distinguish two matrix products:

\[
\mathcal B(X,Y)=R_b(XY),\qquad
\mathcal F(X,Y)=\operatorname{FP32out}(XY),
\]

where both operators receive BF16 operands and use FP32 accumulation.
The notation specifies tensor rounding boundaries; it does not promise a
single GPU kernel or the absence of other temporary buffers.

The reported CUDA runs disable BF16 reduced-precision reductions and set
FP32 matmul precision to ``high''. FP32 denotes tensor and solver
precision; FP32-tensor matrix products inherit that backend setting,
which can use lower-precision internal multiplication. Geometry
diagnostics temporarily use ``highest''. These settings apply to
reference and candidate; casting QR inputs to FP32 does not request
``highest'' matmul precision. The harness also enables deterministic
algorithms; the reproduction guide records the cuBLAS workspace, thread
and compiler-cache settings.

On call \(k=1\), the matrix optimizer initializes zero moments,
accumulates covariances from the clipped gradient, and obtains
descending initial bases by FP32 eigendecomposition of symmetrized
factors. It skips the matrix parameter update. Auxiliary AdamW still
updates on that call. For subsequent calls define the matrix age
\(t=k-1\), and let \(G_b=R_b(G)\) for the current clipped gradient. In
the existing bases,

\[
\bar G=\mathcal B(\mathcal B(Q_L^\top,G_b),Q_R),
\]

\[
M_f=\beta_1\operatorname{float}(M)+(1-\beta_1)\operatorname{float}(\bar G),
\quad
V_f=\beta_2\operatorname{float}(V)+(1-\beta_2)\operatorname{float}(\bar G)^2.
\]

The current direction uses these unrounded FP32 moment temporaries:

\[
U=\mathcal F\!\left(\mathcal B\!\left(Q_L,
 R_b\!\left[\frac{M_f}{\sqrt{V_f}+\epsilon}\right]\right),Q_R^\top\right),
\qquad
W^+=\left(W-\eta_k\frac{\sqrt{1-\beta_2^t}}{1-\beta_1^t}U\right)
 (1-\eta_k\lambda).
\]

The optimizer then stores \(R_b(M_f),R_b(V_f)\), updates the
covariances, and performs any scheduled basis change and moment
transport. Thus the direction precedes this call's basis refresh.
Rounding moments before using them for the current direction would
specify a different implementation.

The tensor-level covariance update is expressed as

\[
C_L^+=R_b\!\left[\beta_C\operatorname{float}(C_L)
 +(1-\beta_C)G_bG_b^\top\right],
\quad
C_R^+=R_b\!\left[\beta_C\operatorname{float}(C_R)
 +(1-\beta_C)G_b^\top G_b\right].
\]

The implementation uses BF16 \texttt{baddbmm} with FP32 accumulation for
this operation. The equations specify the weighted update and its output
dtype; they do not establish the vendor kernel's exact internal rounding
sequence. All main experiments use \(\beta_1=\beta_2=\beta_C=0.95\).

\hypertarget{dense-warm-tracking}{%
\subsection{3.2. Dense warm tracking}\label{dense-warm-tracking}}

For either updated covariance factor \(C\) and its current basis \(Q\),
form (with \(\operatorname{sym}(X)=(X+X^\top)/2\))

\[
B=\operatorname{sym}\bigl(\mathcal F(\mathcal B(Q^\top,C),Q)\bigr),
\quad d_i=B_{ii},\quad
s=\max(\max_i|d_i|,10^{-30}),\quad h_{ij}=(d_i-d_j)/s.
\]

We apply a damped skew motion in the estimated basis coordinates:

\[
A_{ij}=-\frac12\frac{B_{ij}}s\frac{h_{ij}}{h_{ij}^2+0.01^2},
\quad
a=\min\left(1,\frac{0.5}{\max(\|A\|_F/\sqrt2,10^{-12})}\right),
\quad
Y_0=R_b\bigl(\operatorname{float}(Q)+\mathcal F(Q,R_b(aA))\bigr).
\]

Two residual corrections follow:

\[
E_j=\mathcal F(Y_j^\top,Y_j)-I,\qquad
Y_{j+1}=R_b\bigl(\operatorname{float}(Y_j)
 -\tfrac12\mathcal F(Y_j,R_b(E_j))\bigr),\quad j=0,1.
\]

The stored new basis is \(Y_2\). This is an approximate orthogonality
correction, not exact QR. All products are dense: the motivation is GPU
execution and numerical policy, not a reduction in cubic asymptotic
order. The skew direction is a damped first-order Jacobi-type motion
applied to all coordinate pairs. For a small skew \(A\), the unrounded
change is \(B^+\approx B+BA-AB\); its diagonal approximation gives the
gap-dependent off-diagonal correction. Without rounding, each residual
map is \(Y\mapsto(3Y-YY^\top Y)/2\), the cubic Newton--Schulz polar
iteration in the Björck--Bowie family. These primitives themselves are
not new. Before the global cap, the damping weights the undamped
reciprocal-gap formula by \(\tfrac12 h_{ij}^2/(h_{ij}^2+0.01^2)\). It
therefore suppresses motion relative to that formula when
coordinate-diagonal gaps are much smaller than 1\% of \(s\); at equal
diagonals the motion vanishes. These diagonals need not be eigenvalues,
and we did not save spectra or count how many pairs lie in this regime.
The statement does not bound absolute motion.

\hypertarget{qr-repair-and-history-transport}{%
\subsection{3.3. QR repair and history
transport}\label{qr-repair-and-history-transport}}

At positive ages divisible by 10, we refresh both factors. Ages
divisible by 50 use QR repair; the remaining refresh ages use Section
3.2. For QR, cast stored \(C,Q\) to FP32 and reuse one product:

\[
Z=\operatorname{float}(C)\operatorname{float}(Q),\quad
\pi=\operatorname{argsort}_{\downarrow}
 \left(\sum_i\operatorname{float}(Q)_{ij}Z_{ij}\right),\quad
Q^+=R_b\bigl(\operatorname{qr}(Z_{:,\pi}).Q\bigr).
\]

In exact arithmetic the sorting score equals
\(\operatorname{diag}(Q^\top C Q)\), even for nonorthogonal \(Q\). Its
finite-precision evaluation can change near-tie orderings. The existing
reference instead materializes \(Q^\top C Q\) for the diagonal and
separately forms \(CQ_{:,\pi}\), using three dense products rather than
one. QR repair is one warm-started orthogonal power step, not an exact
eigendecomposition. Over 10,000 calls the candidate performs 9,999
matrix updates with 800 warm and 199 QR refreshes; the reference
performs 999 QR refreshes. Auxiliary AdamW performs 10,000 updates in
either arm.

After either refresh, transport the newly rounded first moment by
reconstructing its world-coordinate representation and projecting into
the new bases:

\[
M^{\rm world}=\mathcal B(\mathcal B(Q_L,M),Q_R^\top),\qquad
M^+=\mathcal B(\mathcal B((Q_L^+)^\top,M^{\rm world}),Q_R^+).
\]

Here the unprimed bases are the old bases and \(M\) denotes
\(R_b(M_f)\). QR repair permutes \(R_b(V_f)\) along both axes according
to the sorting orders. Warm refresh leaves that rounded diagonal history
unchanged. Neither choice represents an exact rotation of a full
second-moment tensor. Eschenhagen et al.~report that a basis-aware
second-moment correction hurt in their EShampoo experiments (Appendix
D.2); this motivates care with such corrections but does not validate
our history approximation.

Transpose-based first-moment transport is also approximate when stored
bases are nonorthogonal. QR repair has a substantive recovery role: with
\(Q=I\) and
\(C=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0.5\\0.5&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]\), all
\(h_{ij}=0\), so the warm step leaves the basis unchanged despite
nonzero off-diagonal covariance. This counterexample precludes a claim
that warm motion alone always diagonalizes a factor; it does not
establish incidence in training.

\hypertarget{compilation-and-storage}{%
\subsection{3.4. Compilation and
storage}\label{compilation-and-storage}}

Equal-shaped parameter matrices are batched, retaining independent
covariance, basis, and moment tensors. Four groups, each containing 12
matrices, cover shapes \(2304\times768\), \(768\times768\),
\(3072\times768\), and \(768\times1536\). Both sides of every matrix are
preconditioned: 48 matrices and 96 factors in total. There is no
blocking, one-sided approximation, or coverage change relative to the
reference.

Separate full-graph compiled regions for ordinary, warm, and QR events
include packing parameters/gradients, the matrix update, covariance
accumulation, refresh/transport, and parameter writes. Initial
eigendecompositions are eager. The measured candidate captures 12 graphs
without graph breaks. The compiler uses
\texttt{emulate\_precision\_casts=True}. The saved small synthetic CUDA
parity probe reports zero maximum parameter difference and zero relative
error in all six states for ordinary, warm and QR events. This supports
the requested rounding policy on that probe, not an equality proof for
every training state. FP32 packing buffers and solver workspaces remain
possible.

Per matrix, the six persistent tensors contain \(2mn+2m^2+2n^2\)
elements. BF16 halves their tensor-byte contribution relative to FP32,
while FP32 parameters and auxiliary optimizer state remain. Total GPU
peak allocation falls by a smaller fraction than matrix-state storage in
these measurements (Section 5.2).
