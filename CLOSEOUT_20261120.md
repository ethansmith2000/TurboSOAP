# TurboSOAP close-out (2026-11-20): what we found, and why the project is parked

**Status: parked on 2026-11-20.** No runs are pending or queued. This file is the entry point for anyone
picking the work up again. Detailed evidence lives in the linked reports.

## Bottom line

- **The warm refresh is not cheaper in arithmetic.**
  - Per factor of size n, a warm refresh is about 14n³ flops: seven full n×n BF16 products.
  - A Householder QR refresh is about 4.7n³ flops (Z = CQ, `geqrf`, `orgqr`).
  - Averaged over calls, warm10/QR50 does about 126·d³ refresh flops per layer. QR10 does 49·d³, QR20 24·d³ and
    QR50 10·d³.
- **Its 109M speed advantage comes only from slow QR kernels.** cuSOLVER QR runs at about 4.4 TFLOP/s at the
  109M factor sizes (768–3072). The warm step runs at about 150 TFLOP/s.
  - **Break-even:** warm10/QR50 is cheaper than QR10 only if QR runs below 1/3 of matmul speed. Against QR20 the
    threshold is 1/8; against QR50 it is never cheaper.
  - **Faster QR kernels exist.** The GPU MODE `qr_v2` contest set batched FP32 Householder QR at n = 32–4096 on
    B200.
    - One entrant (12th of 183) reports going from about 108.8 ms to 1.8 ms geometric mean over a `torch.geqrf`
      baseline, about 60×. That is a single write-up whose baseline figures are not fully reconciled
      ([source](https://sankalp.bearblog.dev/autoresearch/)).
    - The techniques were blocked compact-WY updates on tensor cores, Cholesky-QR at n = 4096, and CUDA graphs.
- **The refresh share of step time scales with d/N** (width over tokens per optimizer step).
  - At the 109M setting (32,768 tokens per step), QR10's refresh costs about 30% of a layer's
    forward+backward time and warm10/QR50's about 8%.
  - At ≥ 1M tokens per step with optimizer work split across data-parallel ranks, every schedule costs under 1%
    for d ≥ 768. QR10 is 1.5% and 1.1% at d = 256 and 512.
- **In BF16 at 109M, refresh cadence does not move the 10k endpoint.** QR10, warm10/QR50 and QR50 end within
  0.003 CE of one another on both seeds. Cadence matters early: QR10 leads by about 0.1 CE at call 500, and the
  schedules converge by about call 2000.
- **So the case for the warm tracker is narrow.** It needs high d/N (or unsharded optimizer work), no fast batched
  QR, and training where refresh cadence actually changes the result. The 109M BF16 setting meets only the first
  two.

**Cholesky-QR caveats** (from §2–§4 and refresh study Part 1b):
- The Grams must be IEEE FP32. With TF32 Grams it can silently return a wrong basis, 1.55 rad off.
- In 109M training, 7–10% of factor refreshes fall back to Householder, and every QR event has at least one
  fallback.
- On trained one-layer factors the failure rate rises steeply with n: 11% at d = 2048.
- Its speed edge is gone by n ≈ 4096 (1.07× Householder), and it is 1.9× slower at 8192.

**Context from the literature for finding 9:**
- [FRUGAL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07837) and [Fira](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01623) show how to keep the
  residual outside a low-rank subspace without a scale mismatch.
- The [SOAP paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11321) shows Shampoo (½ power) is Adafactor in Shampoo's
  eigenbasis. So Adafactor in place of per-coordinate Adam mostly recovers Shampoo.

## Not done (parked, in rough priority order)

1. **Why BF16 state cancels the benefit of exact refreshes.** This is the most interesting open question. It
   starts with a gate-qualified FP32-storage QR10 rerun (the runner's own parity routine, 2–3 draws, about
   15 GPU-min each): `reports/fastqr_20261120/REPORT.md` §10.2.
2. **An FP32-state QR50 run to 10k.** It is the missing anchor for whether warm adds endpoint value when the state
   is FP32.
3. **The mechanism of C6's post-switch penalty.** The V-statistics hypothesis is untested. C6 on seed 205 was
   not run.
4. **Width checks.**
   - Timing-only runs at d = 1536 and 2048 (warm10/QR50, QR20, CholQR20, QR10).
   - A loss check at d = 1536 only if those motivate it.
5. **Dimension-free warm-step bounds** (per-row or per-pair bound, noise-floor mask) and the top-k refresh (panel
   C4). Both are open-loop replays on the saved factors. A top-k refresh is not warm-specific: QR can do it too.
6. **One-sided SOAP** (leave the 3d/4d left factors unrotated). By flop count this removes about 87% of refresh
   work and about 60% of the every-call rotation. Untested.
7. **The untied head/embedding study.** Its plan was never approved and nothing was launched beyond a 150-call
   timing probe: `reports/untied_head_20261120/PLAN.md`.

- `paper/TurboSOAP-draft-20261120.pdf` and `paper/overleaf/` include the matched-QR10 integration
  (`paper/edits-20261120*.diff`).
- They do **not** include stages 3–4, a scaling section, Cholesky-QR, or this close-out.
- Two statements in the draft are now out of date:
  - §6's "full-BF16 QR50 ran only to call 2000";
  - §7's open question about whether warm improves on QR50 at the endpoint. Finding 2 answers it: no resolvable
    value.
- `paper/TurboSOAP-Overleaf.zip` predates the 2026-11-20 edits and was not regenerated (`export_overleaf.py` was
  intentionally not run).
- The recommended framing, if it is ever written up, is a short empirical note: "How much eigenbasis refresh does
  SOAP need?" It would cover findings 1–9, with warm presented as a matmul-only approximate refresh.
